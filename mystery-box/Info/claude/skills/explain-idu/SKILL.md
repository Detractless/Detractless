# IDU — explaining something that did not land

## Shape of the reply

**Verdict:** the answer in one sentence.
Then the explanation as prose underneath it.
**Next:** the layer you skipped, offered.

No **Done**, **Not done**, or **Verify** sections — nothing changed.

Bullet caps do not bind the explanation prose. It must still come out **shorter
than the passage that caused the confusion**. If your re-explanation is longer
than what confused them, you have diagnosed wrong.

## Diagnose before writing

Four causes. Only the first is fixed by simplifying:

1. **Missing word** — they hit a term they don't have. Define it, move on.
2. **Skipped step** — the chain had a gap. Fill *that one step*, not the chain.
3. **Too much at once** — same content, reordered, one idea per line.
4. **No sense of why** — state the consequence; skip the mechanism entirely.

Picking cause 1 when it is really cause 2 produces a simpler version of the same
gap, and the user asks again.

## Rules for the explanation

- **Never explain below the knowledge floor** — whatever they already used
  correctly this session, per topic. Re-explaining what they just demonstrated
  reads as condescension.
- **Simplify vocabulary, never facts.** A wrong simple answer is worse than the
  original confusion.
- **One analogy maximum**, and say where it breaks.
- **No toy metaphors** (no jars of marbles, no trains leaving stations).
- **Use their actual thing** — their file, their error, their number — not a
  generic illustration.
- **Stay in the plainer register for that topic** for the rest of the session.
  Sliding back to the original vocabulary two turns later undoes the whole thing.

## Not this protocol

- Frustration ("this still doesn't work") → fix the thing, don't explain.
- "Explain your reasoning" → they want *more* detail, not less.
- "I don't understand why you did that" → an objection to the decision, not a
  request for teaching. Defend or reverse the decision.
